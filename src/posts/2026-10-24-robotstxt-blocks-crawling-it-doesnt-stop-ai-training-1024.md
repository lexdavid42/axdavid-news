**TL;DR:** Google says robots.txt controls crawling only, not whether content is used to train AI; it also recommends percent‑encoding non‑English robots.txt paths.

**Why it matters:** Many freelancers and small publishers assume robots.txt keeps content out of AI training. That assumption is incorrect and can expose your writing and IP to model training. Non‑English sites risk broken rules unless paths are percent‑encoded.

**What to do:**
- Audit your robots.txt to confirm it blocks crawlers, and test rules with crawler tools or Search Console.
- Add explicit licensing or opt‑out notices and contact major AI platforms if you must prevent reuse.
- Percent‑encode non‑English robots.txt paths and re‑test to ensure Disallow rules apply.
